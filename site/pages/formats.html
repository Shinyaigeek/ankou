The output

What comes out

Six shapes. Every one below is embedded live — the same self-contained HTML file kamu writes when you run it, rendered from one fixed source so you can compare them against each other.

These pages were produced by examples/gallery.mjs, which walks every registered format through the renderer with a stub model: no API key, no tokens, no network. The structure, the interactions and the styling are exactly what a real run gives you; only the content is canned — a miniature of Rich Sutton's The Bitter Lesson.

Flipbook

--format flipbook

Plays the source back one character at a time, verbatim, with emphasis and readings. Nothing is summarised or dropped.

  • ParamsbaseMs — milliseconds per character, lower is faster. The planner sets it from how dense the writing is; 40–90 is comfortable.
  • Reach for itReading every single word without skimming. Poetry, contracts, specs — anything where the wording is the point.
  • In a browserFull-screen character stage with a running context strip. space plays, ←→ step (hold shift for 20), ↑↓ change speed, c opens the chapter sidebar.
  • In a terminalThe same tape, revealed into wrapped text with the current character marked.

The model only annotates — chapter titles, emphasis terms, ruby readings, pacing. Punctuation and paragraph breaks get longer pauses than ordinary characters, and emphasised terms are slowed slightly so they register.

Slides

--format slides

A deck: one claim per slide with supporting bullets and speaker notes. Good for grasping the shape of an argument fast.

  • ParamsslideCount, tone.
  • Reach for itGetting the gist quickly, presenting the material to someone else, or reviewing something you have read before.
  • In a browserKeyboard deck: ←→ to page, n toggles speaker notes, f fullscreen, Home/End to jump. Slides deep-link by hash, and printing gives one slide per page with the notes visible.

Every headline is a claim the source makes, not a label — "Computation beats cleverness, given time", not "Background". Each slide may carry one focal element: a verbatim quote, a stat, a code block, or mermaid source. 

kamu paper.pdf -i "brief the team" \
  --format slides --param slideCount=12 --open

Quiz

--format quiz

Questions with answers, explanations and a quote from the source. Tests whether the material actually stuck.

  • ParamsquestionCount, difficulty — 1 recall, 2 understanding, 3 application.
  • Reach for itRevision, exam or interview preparation, or checking your understanding after a read.
  • In a browserAnswer, submit, get graded, see what you missed and why.
  • In a terminalThe same, one question at a time, with a score at the end.

Single-choice, multi-choice and short answer. Short answers are compared after normalising case, whitespace (including full-width) and trailing punctuation, against a list of accepted strings — so a right answer typed slightly differently still counts. 

kamu https://example.com/post -i "覚えたい" --play
# the terminal player grades you as you go

Animation

--format animation

Kinetic typography: the ideas arrive as timed, moving text. Cinematic rather than exhaustive.

  • ParamssceneCount, pace — slow, medium or fast.
  • Reach for itMaking an idea land emotionally. A manifesto, a story, a pitch — or something you want other people to actually watch.
  • In a browserAuto-advancing player. Click or space to pause, ←→ to step, f fullscreen.

Scenes of one to four lines, each with a motion — fade, slide-up, zoom, typewriter, stagger, flip — a duration, a hold, and a mood that sets the palette. This is the one format allowed to be selective: it is cutting a trailer, not writing a report.

Summary

--format summary

A layered digest: TL;DR, key points, a per-section outline, a glossary and the questions left open.

  • Paramsdepth — brief or thorough.
  • Reach for itDeciding whether the full document is worth your time, or keeping a reference you will come back to.
  • In a browserA document you can print or keep.

Map-reduce over the document: per-window digests first, then one final pass for the TL;DR and key points — so the top level reflects the whole thing rather than whatever happened to be in the first window. 

kamu report.pdf -i "worth my afternoon?" \
  --format summary --param depth=brief

Dialogue

--format dialogue

Two voices working through the material — one explaining, one asking the questions you would ask.

  • Paramslevel — beginner, intermediate or expert.
  • Reach for itMaterial you keep bouncing off, or when you want the why behind something rather than the what.
  • In a browserThe transcript is revealed one turn at a time: space for the next turn, a to reveal all.

The two speakers are established in the first window and reused across the rest, so the voices stay consistent over a long document. The learner asks the awkward question and pushes back on hand-waving — which is what surfaces the objections a straight explanation glosses over.

Choosing

You do not have to pick

Leave --format off and the planner picks from your intent, then tells you why in your own language. --plan-only shows that decision — one cheap model call — before anything is generated. 

kamu spec.md -i "レビューの前に頭に入れたい" --plan-only
# format:    summary
# rationale: レビュー前の確認なので、全体の主張と論点を…

kamu formats prints the same list you just read, generated from the format registry — so it cannot drift from what is installed.

Adding one

A format is one file

Export a FormatModule from packages/core/src/formats/<id>.ts, add its Piece variant, register it, and give it a renderer branch. The test suite walks every registered format through every renderer, so a new one is covered the moment it is registered.

Its description and bestFor are not documentation: they are the planner's menu. Write them for the model as much as for the reader.
